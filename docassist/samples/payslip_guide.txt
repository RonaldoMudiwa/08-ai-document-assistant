Understanding Your Payslip

This is a made up example document for trying the program. It is a simple guide, not financial advice.

Your gross pay is everything you earn before anything is taken off. It includes your basic pay, overtime, commission and bonuses.

Your net pay is what is left after the deductions. This is the amount that reaches your bank account. It is sometimes called take home pay.

Income tax is taken off through PAYE, which stands for Pay As You Earn. Your employer takes the tax off before you are paid and sends it to HMRC.

Your tax code tells your employer how much you can earn before you start paying tax. The most common tax code is 1257L. If your tax code looks wrong, contact HMRC, because you could be paying too much tax.

National Insurance is also taken off your pay. Paying it builds up your right to the State Pension and some benefits.

Pension contributions come out of your pay each month if you are in your employer's workplace pension scheme. Your employer adds money to your pension too.

Student loan repayments are taken off if you earn over the threshold for your repayment plan.

Payslips are usually given out on payday. Keep them for at least a year, because you may need them to rent a flat, get a mortgage or check your tax.
